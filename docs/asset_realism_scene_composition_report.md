These packages are not project-owned.

## What export and ingestion changed

For the desk, shelf, floor, table, and bowl, the OBJ polygon counts match the counts stored beside the source blend. Those OBJ files have no `vt`, no `vn`, and no `usemtl`. The texture directories are empty. So the appearance was already absent from the historical OBJ extract. That is a packaging loss, not a measurement that the source blend was a box. The source audit is `dataset/qualification/asset_realism/source_audit/source_audit.md`.

The Infinigen 2 preview Mitsuba PLY export is different. `sofa.ply` has 1902 vertices, 1296 faces, normals, and `s`/`t` coordinates. `lamp-material_52.ply` has normals and UVs. The new importer `infinigen_ingest.py` keeps those UVs and normals. It does not call `publish_candidate`. Packages are under `dataset/qualification/asset_realism/packages/`.

`qual_sofa` and `qual_lamp` are the Infinigen meshes. `qual_desk`, `qual_shelf`, `qual_table`, and `qual_bowl` are the historical OBJs, still without UVs, because that is what the OBJ extract contained. A Mitsuba pair on the sofa, `mitsuba_sofa_base_on.png` and `mitsuba_sofa_base_off.png`, uses a qualification base-color bitmap on those UVs. The blue and red split is visible, so the scientific path consumes the map. The bitmap is an authored probe. It is not the original Infinigen shader, which was not in the extract as an image. Metallic, ambient occlusion, height, and opacity stay `unsupported` on this pin. `texture_binding` records that status. Roughness still converts once to Beckmann alpha. Filtering stays bilinear. `trilinear` and `ewa` stay rejected.

## Frames, proxies, and scenes

Semantic frames use `right = forward × up`. A reversed right axis fails. The monitor screen normal is the min-Y screen face, not the long axis of the box. The sofa forward points from the backrest toward the seat front. Collision proxies use `stride_keep_support` and are not the mesh Mitsuba loads. Deviation tolerance is 0.5 percent of the largest dimension, and at least 1 mm.

Twelve scenes, two sizes each of living, office, kitchen, bedroom, dining, and workshop, are in `dataset/qualification/asset_realism/scenes/`. Room shells come from `make_specification` (called, not edited) plus a floor and ceiling written only in the qualification directory. The Infinigen `room_shape` extract is a 96-triangle shell and is not used as the floor. Every accepted scene has `critic.valid` true. The saved proposal `rejected_sofa_reversed` has `valid` false with failure reason `facing` on the sofa. The solver version is `anneal_yaw_v1`. It does not change scale, pitch, or roll. Replay is the saved `scene.json`: package ids, hashes, and transforms. It does not run the solver again.

Gap categories, because the pinned extracts do not contain them, are chair (two widths), monitor (two heights), television, bed, refrigerator, microwave, cabinet, nightstand, wardrobe, keyboard, mouse, and toolbox. They are component assemblies (legs, seat, back, door, screen, handle), generated by `gap_assets.py`, and labeled `sensor_sim` rather than Infinigen. They are not a claim that a few boxes match an Infinigen sofa. The sofa in the living-room scenes is the Infinigen mesh.

## What Mitsuba still does not match

The original Infinigen procedural shaders were not image files in the OBJ extract, so the Mitsuba furniture color is either the scalar diffuse used in the room renders or the authored probe on the sofa. Slot names such as `shader_shelves_black_wood.049` were not bound. A normal-map change would be shading only. Polarization accuracy was not measured.
